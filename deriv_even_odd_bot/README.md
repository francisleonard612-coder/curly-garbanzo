# Deriv Digit Even/Odd Quantitative Prediction & Trading Engine

A production-grade selective trading engine for Deriv digit Even/Odd
contracts. Built to the attached 72-section specification.

The governing principle, from Section 72:

> **WAIT FOR EVIDENCE. TRADE ONLY WHEN THE CALIBRATED PROBABILITY,
> UNCERTAINTY, MODEL AGREEMENT, AND ACTUAL CONTRACT ECONOMICS ALL SUPPORT
> THE SAME DECISION.**

---

## Read this before running it

This engine is a measurement instrument first and a trading bot second.
On Deriv's synthetic indices — generated by an audited CSPRNG — the expected
and correct behaviour is that **it never trades**.

Two numbers explain why, and no amount of modelling changes them:

| Quantity | Value |
|---|---|
| Even/Odd payout (typical) | ~1.95x |
| Break-even accuracy required | **51.28%** |
| Accuracy achievable on a fair digit stream | **50.00%** |

The 1.28-point gap is the house edge. The engine's job is to establish,
continuously and rigorously, whether the fair-stream assumption actually
holds for the stream it is watching — and to trade only if it demonstrably
does not.

### What the Section 2–19 rewrite changed

The previous version reached that conclusion by **assumption**: a randomness
battery held an absolute veto, so a stream that tested fair was refused at
step 4 of an 11-deep gate chain and no quote was ever fetched. Every
rejection collapsed into one reason code, `NO_TRADE_RANDOM_STREAM`, and the
bot was indistinguishable from a broken one.

It now reaches the same conclusion by **measurement**. Randomness and regime
are continuous evidence (Sections 3, 4); the eleven binary cutoffs are one
weighted opportunity score (Sections 6, 13); every candidate is priced
against a real proposal and recorded with its edge attached.

Verified on a 28,500-candidate CSPRNG run:

```
quoted 28,500   traded 0

reason codes    NO_TRADE_NEGATIVE_EV          74%
                NO_TRADE_LOW_EDGE             16%
                NO_TRADE_CALIBRATION_UNFITTED 10%

mean point edge -0.0051 at 1.950x   (break-even 0.5128, models averaging 0.5077)

DEADLOCK   This is a pricing gap, not a configuration problem — lowering a
           threshold cannot make a negative-EV contract profitable.
SHADOW     No threshold in the scanned range admits a single positive-EV
           candidate, so the cutoff is not what is holding the bot back.
```

Same decision as before. Completely different epistemic status.

### The thing to understand before touching a threshold

On a fair stream, roughly **1% of scored candidates still cross the base
opportunity threshold**, and their mean *point* edge is +0.036. Those are
sampling-noise excursions, not signal — a 5,000-sample estimate of a 0.5
process wanders above 0.5128 constantly.

They do not trade because adaptive selectivity (Section 15) raises the
required score to a mean of 92.9 for exactly those candidates: an unusually
extreme model claim coincides with degraded calibration and high dispersion,
which is what the multiplier responds to. That is a probabilistic defence,
not arithmetic, and it is the reason the thresholds are where they are.

**Lowering `min_opportunity_score` does not find edge. It admits noise.**
The shadow report exists so that this is a measured statement rather than an
opinion — check it before changing anything.

## Trading modes (Sections 64–66)

Real money requires **two independent switches to agree**. Disagreement is
a hard startup failure, never a silent downgrade.

| `TRADING_MODE` | `DERIV_USE_REAL` | Behaviour |
|---|---|---|
| `research` | `false` | Full pipeline, dashboard, logging. **BUY is never called.** |
| `demo` | `false` | Real order flow against demo balance. |
| `live` | `true` | Real money. Both switches required. |
| `live` | `false` | **Refuses to start.** |
| anything | `true` | **Refuses to start** unless mode is `live`. |

### The prediction ordering (Section 22)

Leakage is the failure that produces a beautiful backtest and a losing
live account. The ordering in `app/execution/engine.py` is fixed:

```
1. features   = build(state)      # state does NOT contain the tick yet
2. prediction = models.predict()  # prediction is ABOUT that tick
3. ... gate, maybe trade ...
4. state.add(tick)                # only now observed
5. models.observe(digit)          # label revealed
```

Step 4 must never move above step 2.

## Design decisions worth knowing

**Digit extraction is decimal-safe.** `str(1234.30)[-1]` returns `3` for a
price whose real final digit is `0`. Because a stripped trailing zero
always replaces an *even* digit, that bug is a systematic parity bias in
exactly the series being traded. Extraction uses `Decimal` quantized to the
instrument's own `pip_size`, with `ROUND_HALF_UP` (Python's default
banker's rounding also biases toward even digits).

**Expected value is the one economic condition that stays hard.** Section 3
softens the randomness veto because "is there structure?" is a question about
evidence and belongs on a continuum. It does not soften the economics,
because "does this contract pay more than it costs?" is arithmetic on the
quote Deriv just sent. Section 14 classifies a candidate with no economically
valid edge as INVALID, and `hard_gates.check_execution` enforces it.

**The lower confidence bound moved from veto to score.** Section 10 says
requiring it to clear break-even in *every* situation is too restrictive, so
`conservative_edge` is now a weighted contributor with a floor rather than a
cutoff. Point estimates wander above break-even constantly and lower bounds
do not — that asymmetry is still the main defence against the
multiple-comparisons problem, it just expresses itself as lost score now
instead of a deleted candidate.

**Mutual information is bias-corrected.** The plug-in MI estimator is
positive in expectation even at true MI = 0; for digit pairs the bias is
~81/(2n), so 1,000 ticks show ~0.04 nats of "dependence" in pure noise
every time. Miller-Madow correction plus a proper chi-square test are
applied; raw MI is never used alone.

**Pattern discovery is guarded four ways.** Enumerating last-1..4 digit
contexts is 11,110 hypotheses; at α=0.05 that is ~555 "significant"
findings from noise. Benjamini-Hochberg FDR + minimum support +
out-of-sample confirmation + economic screening. Verified: zero actionable
patterns across five independent 60k-digit CSPRNG scans.

**Markov order is chosen by BIC.** A 2nd-order digit chain has 900 free
parameters. On independent data BIC correctly collapses to order 0.

**Run-length analysis does not assume gambler's fallacy.** It *measures*
P(parity | run length) and reports it flat when it is flat — which
empirically refutes the fallacy rather than encoding it.

**The opportunity score now decides, and it has floors.** The old
`trade_quality_score` was reporting-only, on the correct reasoning that an
unvalidated weighted sum should not decide anything. Sections 6 and 13
require the opposite, because eleven stacked cutoffs pass ~3% of genuine
opportunities and the survivors are chosen by whichever cutoff is tightest
rather than by merit. The resolution is that the blend is **additive** (so no
single weak metric zeroes a strong opportunity) but each contributor declares
a `floor_at`, and falling below it **caps** the final score rather than
vetoing — a capped candidate lands in BORDERLINE, visible and diagnosed,
instead of being silently deleted from the statistics.

**Adaptive selectivity is one-directional, by construction.** Degrading model
health raises the required score, up to 1.6x. Nothing lowers it — in
particular a long dry spell does not, which is the single most tempting bug
to write in a bot that is not trading, because the thing the operator wants
is one constant away. `DeadlockMonitor` has no write path to any threshold.

**Martingale is off by default and separate from prediction.** At negative
expectancy it converts a slow bleed into a fast one. A 12-loss streak was
observed live in a sibling bot — at factor 2.0 that is a 4,096x stake.

## Carried forward from prior production failures

| Failure | Defence |
|---|---|
| Recv pump crashed on keepalive timeout; ticks silently stopped forever while the process looked healthy | `_supervise_pump()` watches the pump task and reconnects on any exit |
| First fix resubscribed *before* restarting the reader, so every resubscribe hung for the full timeout, deterministically | `_reconnect()` starts the pump **before** the resubscribe loop |
| Balance-scaled stake and risk allocator disagreed by 527x | `base_stake` is a fixed amount; Kelly/percentage take balance explicitly and are capped by `max_stake` |
| Calibrator never fit, so "raw AND calibrated both clear threshold" was one number checked twice | `is_fitted` is public and gated on |
| Quality gate created a permanent self-sustaining lockout | probe mechanism, streak resets only on a real settlement |
| Decay-ceiling: effective counts approach `1/(1-decay)` from below and never reach it, pinning gates at zero forever | documented in `tick_store.py`; `effective_n` and `ceiling` both exposed |
| Retried buy opened two contracts | idempotency key + `BuyAmbiguousError` forces reconciliation, never retry |
| Legacy connect-then-authorize produced handshake 401s | REST OTP exchange for a pre-authenticated WS URL (`DERIV_AUTH_MODE=otp`) |
| `proposal`/`buy`/`proposal_open_contract`/`sell` share ONE 360/min budget, not 360 each; dozens of rate-limit errors in a single second | client-side pacing to 300/min before sending |
| Settlement polled every 1s for up to 120s — 120 requests against that shared budget for one position | subscribe once, consume pushes |
| websockets 14+ removed `.closed`; reading it raised AttributeError right after every successful connect | `.state is WsState.OPEN` throughout |
| Current Options API renamed `symbol` → `underlying_symbol` and rejects `product_type` | both handled; `barrier` omitted for DIGITEVEN/DIGITODD |
| A trailing newline in a pasted API token 401s identically to a revoked one | settings strip; auth errors report token shape, never the token |
| **CONFIRMED LIVE 2026-09-16**, real demo account: `contracts_for` rejects `currency` (`Properties not allowed: currency`), which crash-looped a Railway deployment before a single tick was subscribed | `currency` dropped from the request; the Section 1 fail-safe worked exactly as designed — it just needed the right request to check against |
| Same incident, a second bug: a *request failure* on `contracts_for` and a *confirmed* "Even/Odd not offered" both read as `(False, "contracts_for failed: ...")`, so a live log looked like Deriv said R_75/R_100 lack Even/Odd, when actually the check never completed | the two cases now say so explicitly — `"REQUEST FAILED ... never confirmed either way"` vs `"Deriv CONFIRMED missing contract types"` |
| Martingale existed in `StakingEngine` but was never read from config or env — `method: martingale` alone did nothing | wired end to end; `config.yaml` ships it on (trigger 2, max 3 steps, factor 2.0), with a startup warning if `martingale_max_steps` sits too close to the hard `max_consecutive_losses` stop |
| A settlement timeout was recorded as a loss in the trades table but did NOT update the martingale loss-streak counter — the DB and the live escalation state could disagree | both now update together on a timeout, conservatively, as a loss |
| `emergency_stop` is a method and `emergency_stopped` is the flag; reading the former gives a truthy bound method and freezes the bot permanently | found by the smoke harness during this rewrite — 3,000/3,000 ticks refused with `NO_TRADE_EMERGENCY_STOP`; now reads the flag |

## Deliberate omissions

**Neural model (Section 8, Model G): not implemented.** Section 8 says it
"must prove incremental predictive value" and Section 72 says complexity
must earn its place. The linear and tree models already sit at chance;
adding a GRU to fit the same noise with more parameters would violate
Sections 42 and 51. The ablation harness (`app/backtest/simulator.py`) is
the mechanism for justifying it — if a non-chance signal ever appears that
a linear model cannot capture, add one then, with evidence.

**SHAP (Section 52): permutation importance is implemented instead.** Same
diagnostic purpose, no extra dependency, and importance is explicitly not
treated as causal.

## Where a real edge could come from

Not from better models. Break-even is a function of the quoted payout
alone:

| Payout | Accuracy needed to break even |
|---|---|
| 1.88x | 53.19% |
| 1.95x | 51.28% |
| 1.97x | 50.76% |
| 2.00x | 50.00% |

If the digit stream is fair, none of these is profitable. But payout
selection is the only lever that moves the target without requiring the
models to be smarter — and if a genuine departure from randomness ever
appears, it is what converts that departure into money.

Railway + Supabase: see **[DEPLOY.md](DEPLOY.md)**. Schema in

optional — **replicas must stay at 1**, and a container that restarts every few
hours will never trade, because every restart resets the calibrator and
`NO_TRADE_CALIBRATION_UNFITTED` is a hard gate. That failure looks exactly like
a deadlock. Check the `events` table before touching a threshold.

## Do the opportunity weights earn their values?

They are my judgement until measured. `app/backtest/simulator.py` is the
measurement: it drives the real pipeline and the real gate, records the
opportunity score for **every** candidate along with the counterfactual
outcome, and reports win rate by score decile plus each contributor's
correlation with outcome.

The test that matters is not the mean correlation, it is **sign consistency
across walk-forward blocks**. On a CSPRNG stream:

```
score/outcome correlation per block: -0.0208, +0.0322, -0.0395
correlation stability (sd across blocks): 0.0372
  -> SIGN FLIPS ACROSS BLOCKS. The opportunity score has no reproducible
     relationship to outcome here. Do NOT tune weights on this.
```

A small standard deviation around a flipping sign is low-variance noise, not
evidence. The harness reports it that way on purpose, because a weight fitted
to the one favourable block is fitted to that block's noise.

The simulator cannot deadlock or pollute the live bot: it refuses a pipeline
whose gate is marked `_live`, builds a fresh pipeline per walk-forward block,
and writes no thresholds. There are tests for each of those three.

## Disclaimers

This software does not guarantee profitability and makes no claim to
predict random outcomes. It is designed to determine whether a tradeable
edge exists and to decline to trade when one does not. On an audited
CSPRNG-driven instrument, declining is the expected result.

Trading binary options carries substantial risk of loss.
